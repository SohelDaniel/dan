# Calculus: the derivative as the limit of secant slopes.
title "The derivative is a limit"
schema axes x=[-0.5,3] y=[-1,9]

mark f   path fn={x^2} label="f(x) = x²"
mark h   label value=1 label="h"
mark m   label value={(f(1+h) - f(1)) / h} label="secant slope"
mark p   dot x=1 y={f(1)} label="P"
mark q   dot x={1+h} y={f(1+h)} label="Q"
mark sec line x1=1 y1={f(1)} x2={1+h} y2={f(1+h)} extend=1 label="secant"

Say: "Take two points on the curve, P and Q, a distance h apart."
focus sec
Say: "The line through them is a secant. Its slope is three."
focus q
Say: "Now let h shrink toward zero, |shrink| and watch Q slide down to P."
@shrink morph h.value 1 -> 0.01 4s ease=smooth
@shrink link close similar q -- p
focus m
Say: "The secant slope settles at two."
mark tan line x=1 y={f(1)} slope={deriv(f, 1)} label="tangent"
focus tan
Say: "That limiting line is the tangent. Its slope, two, is the derivative of x squared at one."
focus none
